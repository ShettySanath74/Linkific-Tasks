# Research Proposal

## Title
**Data Science vs. Decision Science: From Predicting Outcomes to Making Better Business Decisions**

## Background
Businesses use data to understand situations, estimate outcomes, and choose actions. Data Science commonly emphasizes extracting insights and developing descriptive, diagnostic, or predictive analyses. Decision Science emphasizes structuring choices and evaluating actions against objectives and constraints. The fields overlap: predictions can inform decisions, and decision analysis can use data and models.

## Research question
How do Data Science and Decision Science differ in their goals, methods, and roles in business decision-making, and how can they complement each other?

## Objectives
- Describe typical goals and questions in each field.
- Compare methods, outputs, evaluation, and business applications.
- Identify overlap and complementary roles.
- Demonstrate both perspectives using one illustrative business case.
- Discuss limitations without claiming one field is universally better.

## Working hypothesis
The fields have different primary emphases—analyzing data and estimating outcomes versus evaluating choices and consequences—but combining them can provide more complete decision support. This is a proposition to examine, not a predetermined result.

## Scope and method
The project focuses on business decision-making. It uses a source-based comparison and a simplified marketing case with synthetic data. The approach is a focused literature review, structured comparison, illustrative case analysis, and synthesis.

## Expected outcome
A balanced, source-supported comparison of the fields, their overlap, and how they can work together.

## Limitations
Definitions vary across organizations. The example is simplified and synthetic, so it cannot establish general performance differences.
